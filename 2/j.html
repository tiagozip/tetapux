Malware Warning

This page summarizes immediate steps, dos & don'ts, and trusted resources if you suspect infection.

Severity
High

Overview

Malware can range from annoying adware to dangerous ransomware and data stealers. If you see unexpected behavior (popups, encrypted files, unknown processes, or blocked access), treat the situation as potentially serious.

Immediate actions (first 10 minutes)

  • Disconnect from the network (unplug Ethernet, turn off Wi‑Fi) to limit spread and exfiltration.
  • Isolate the affected device — don't use it for banking or passwords.
  • Do not pay ransom, click suspicious links, or provide credentials to unknown contacts.
  • Take photos/screenshots of any ransom messages or alerts for evidence.
Why disconnect?

Disconnecting can stop immediate data transmission, lateral movement across your network, and further damage while you assess the incident.

Next steps & recommended checks

  • Run a full system scan with a reputable antivirus/antimalware product. If unsure, seek professional help rather than running unknown tools.
  • Change passwords from a clean device for critical accounts (email, banking) and enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Back up important files (if not encrypted) to an external drive that you can keep offline.
  • Collect basic incident details: time, symptoms, filenames, screenshots, and any unusual processes or network traffic.
  • Consider contacting a professional incident responder for suspected data theft or ransomware.

What NOT to do

  • Do not follow instructions from unknown popups or messages instructing you to install software.
  • Do not give remote access to unverified "tech support" callers.
  • Do not post sensitive screenshots publicly (they may contain personal data).

Trusted resources

  • US-CERT (CISA)
  • Microsoft Support - Security
  • FTC - Avoid Phishing

Printable checklist

  1. Isolate device from network
  2. Document symptoms and take screenshots
  3. Scan with trusted AV/antimalware
  4. Change critical passwords from a safe device
  5. Contact IT/security professional if needed
If this is an emergency affecting many users or critical systems, contact your IT/security team and local authorities. This page is informational and not a substitute for professional incident response.
