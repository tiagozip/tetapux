Silicon Valley's Secret Nootropic: The $2 Pill CEOs Use Instead of Adderall

A Stanford-developed cognitive enhancer is quietly replacing prescription stimulants among tech executives. The FDA is under pressure to regulate it — or ban it entirely.
By James Whitfield, Forbes Contributor
— · 9 min read

There's an open secret in Sand Hill Road offices and Y Combinator batch dinners: almost nobody is on Adderall anymore.

Instead, a growing number of Silicon Valley's elite — founders, VCs, and senior engineers — have quietly switched to a nootropic compound called NeuraFlow, originally developed by Stanford's Department of Neuroscience as part of a DARPA-funded research project on cognitive performance under sleep deprivation.

The FDA Dilemma

Because NeuraFlow is classified as a dietary supplement rather than a pharmaceutical, it doesn't require FDA approval. But that may be changing. Senator Maria Cantwell (D-WA) has introduced a bill that would require cognitive enhancers to undergo the same clinical trial process as prescription drugs.

"We're creating a two-tier cognitive society," Cantwell said in a press conference. "The wealthy get chemical advantages while everyone else falls behind. That's not acceptable."

NeuraFlow's founder, who operates the company from a Cayman Islands holding structure, has reportedly hired three former FDA commissioners as lobbyists.

Whether NeuraFlow represents a genuine breakthrough in cognitive science or an elaborate supplement marketing operation remains to be seen. But one thing is clear: in a town obsessed with competitive advantage, the demand for cognitive enhancement isn't going away.
